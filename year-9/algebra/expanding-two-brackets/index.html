Year 9 · Algebra

Expanding two brackets

What you'll learn

  • I can expand two brackets such as \((x + 3)(x + 5)\) using a grid.
  • I can expand brackets that contain subtraction, such as \((x - 4)(x + 2)\).
  • I can simplify my answer by collecting like terms.
  • I can expand a squared bracket such as \((x + 4)^2\).

Key ideas

Key idea

Expanding means multiplying out the brackets. Every term in the first bracket multiplies every term in the second bracket.

Two terms times two terms gives four products. Then you collect any like terms.

Why it works: the area model

Think of \((x + 3)(x + 5)\) as the area of a rectangle that is \(x + 3\) tall and \(x + 5\) wide. Split it into four smaller rectangles and add up their areas.

The grid method

The same idea as a table. Put one bracket across the top and the other down the side, then multiply to fill each box.

\((x + 3)(x + 5)\)
\(\times\)\(x\)\(+5\)
\(x\)\(x^2\)\(+5x\)
\(+3\)\(+3x\)\(+15\)

Add the four boxes and collect the like terms \(5x\) and \(3x\):

\[ (x + 3)(x + 5) = x^2 + 8x + 15 \]

Keep the sign with each term. In \((x - 4)\) the second term is \(-4\), not \(4\).

Rule \[ (a + b)(c + d) = ac + ad + bc + bd \]
Squared brackets

A squared bracket means the bracket times itself: \((x + 4)^2 = (x + 4)(x + 4)\). Write it out as two brackets, then expand as normal.

Worked examples

Both terms adding

Expand and simplify \((x + 2)(x + 6)\).

Show solution
  1. Multiply every term by every term: \(x \times x = x^2\), \(x \times 6 = 6x\), \(2 \times x = 2x\), \(2 \times 6 = 12\).
  2. Write them out: \(x^2 + 6x + 2x + 12\).
  3. Collect like terms: \(6x + 2x = 8x\).

\((x + 2)(x + 6) = x^2 + 8x + 12\)

One negative term

Expand and simplify \((x + 5)(x - 3)\).

Show solution
\(\times\)\(x\)\(-3\)
\(x\)\(x^2\)\(-3x\)
\(+5\)\(+5x\)\(-15\)
  1. Add the boxes: \(x^2 - 3x + 5x - 15\).
  2. Collect like terms: \(-3x + 5x = 2x\).

\((x + 5)(x - 3) = x^2 + 2x - 15\)

Two negative terms

Expand and simplify \((x - 4)(x - 7)\).

Show solution
  1. Multiply every term by every term: \(x \times x = x^2\), \(x \times (-7) = -7x\), \((-4) \times x = -4x\), \((-4) \times (-7) = +28\).
  2. Write them out: \(x^2 - 7x - 4x + 28\).
  3. Collect like terms: \(-7x - 4x = -11x\).

\((x - 4)(x - 7) = x^2 - 11x + 28\)

A negative times a negative is positive, so the number at the end is \(+28\).

A squared bracket

Expand and simplify \((x + 3)^2\).

Show solution
  1. Write it as two brackets: \((x + 3)(x + 3)\).
  2. Multiply every term by every term: \(x^2 + 3x + 3x + 9\).
  3. Collect like terms: \(3x + 3x = 6x\).

\((x + 3)^2 = x^2 + 6x + 9\)

Common mistakes

Common mistake

\((x + 3)^2\) is not \(x^2 + 9\). Squaring the bracket means \((x + 3)(x + 3)\), which has a middle term: \(x^2 + 6x + 9\).

Common mistake

Losing a negative sign. In \((x - 4)(x - 7)\), the last product is \((-4) \times (-7) = +28\), not \(-28\). Write each sign in the grid before you multiply.
