Pythagoras' theorem

What you'll learn

  • I can identify the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle, whichever way round it is drawn.
  • I can state Pythagoras' theorem and explain why it works.
  • I can use Pythagoras' theorem to find the hypotenuse.
  • I can use Pythagoras' theorem to find a shorter side.
  • I can decide whether a triangle is right-angled from its side lengths.
  • I can use Pythagoras' theorem to solve problems, including distances on a grid and problems with two steps.

Key words

Right angle
An angle of exactly \(90^\circ\), shown with a small square.
Hypotenuse
The longest side of a right-angled triangle. It is always opposite the right angle.
Square number
A number multiplied by itself, such as \(5^2 = 25\).
Square root
The number that was squared to give a result: \(\sqrt{25} = 5\).
Theorem
A mathematical statement that has been proved to always be true.
Pythagorean triple
Three whole numbers that fit Pythagoras' theorem exactly, such as 3, 4, 5.

1. Right-angled triangles and the hypotenuse

Pythagoras' theorem is about right-angled triangles only. The first job in every question is to find the right angle, and then the hypotenuse.

Key idea

The hypotenuse is the side opposite the right angle. It is the side that does not touch the right-angle box, and it is always the longest side.

Triangles are not always drawn with the right angle in the bottom corner. Turn the page in your head until the right angle is at the bottom left, or simply look for the side that does not touch the right-angle box.

2. Pythagoras' theorem

Pythagoras' theorem \[ a^2 + b^2 = c^2 \]

In a right-angled triangle, the squares of the two shorter sides add up to the square of the hypotenuse.

Why it works

Draw a square on each side of a triangle with sides 3, 4 and 5. The two smaller squares have a total area equal to the big square.

This is not a coincidence. It works for every right-angled triangle, and it has been proved in hundreds of different ways. One of the most famous proofs is below.

Pythagorean triples

Some right-angled triangles have whole-number sides. These sets of three numbers are called Pythagorean triples. They are worth recognising because they appear in many questions.

3. Finding the hypotenuse

To find the hypotenuse, add the squares of the two shorter sides, then take the square root.

Finding the hypotenuse \[ c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} \]
Method
  1. Label the hypotenuse \(c\) (opposite the right angle).
  2. Write \(c^2 = a^2 + b^2\) and substitute.
  3. Work out the right-hand side.
  4. Square root to find \(c\). Round if needed, and include units.
  5. Check: is \(c\) the longest side?

Whole-number answer

Find the length \(c\).

Show solution
  1. \(c\) is opposite the right angle, so it is the hypotenuse. Add the squares.
  2. \(c^2 = 6^2 + 8^2 = 36 + 64 = 100\)
  3. \(c = \sqrt{100} = 10\)

\(c = 10\) cm

Decimal answer

A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 3.2 cm and 5.7 cm. Find the length of the hypotenuse, correct to 3 significant figures.

Show solution
  1. \(c^2 = 3.2^2 + 5.7^2 = 10.24 + 32.49 = 42.73\)
  2. \(c = \sqrt{42.73} = 6.5368\ldots\)
  3. Check: \(6.54\) is longer than both \(3.2\) and \(5.7\). ✓

\(c = 6.54\) cm (3 s.f.)

Write down a few more digits than you need before rounding, so the examiner can see your working.

Exact answers

If a question asks for an exact answer, leave the square root in: \(c = \sqrt{74}\) cm rather than \(8.60\) cm. You will learn to simplify answers like these in Surds (Year 10).

4. Finding a shorter side

If you know the hypotenuse and one shorter side, rearrange the theorem: subtract the square of the shorter side from the square of the hypotenuse, then square root.

Finding a shorter side \[ a = \sqrt{c^2 - b^2} \]

Whole-number answer

Find the length \(a\).

Show solution
  1. The hypotenuse is 13 cm, so \(a\) is a shorter side. Subtract the squares.
  2. \(a^2 = 13^2 - 5^2 = 169 - 25 = 144\)
  3. \(a = \sqrt{144} = 12\)

\(a = 12\) cm

Decimal answer

The hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle is 9.5 m and one of the shorter sides is 4.1 m. Find the other side, correct to 3 significant figures.

Show solution
  1. We know the hypotenuse, so subtract: \(a^2 = 9.5^2 - 4.1^2 = 90.25 - 16.81 = 73.44\)
  2. \(a = \sqrt{73.44} = 8.5697\ldots\)
  3. Check: \(8.57\) is shorter than the hypotenuse \(9.5\). ✓

\(a = 8.57\) m (3 s.f.)

5. Is the triangle right-angled?

Pythagoras' theorem also works backwards. If the two shorter sides squared add up to the longest side squared, the triangle must have a right angle. If they don't, it doesn't.

Testing a triangle

A triangle has sides 7 cm, 24 cm and 25 cm. Is it a right-angled triangle?

Show solution
  1. If it is right-angled, the longest side (25 cm) is the hypotenuse.
  2. Shorter sides: \(7^2 + 24^2 = 49 + 576 = 625\)
  3. Longest side: \(25^2 = 625\)

They are equal, so yes, the triangle is right-angled.

Not right-angled

A triangle has sides 6 cm, 9 cm and 11 cm. Is it a right-angled triangle?

Show solution
  1. Shorter sides: \(6^2 + 9^2 = 36 + 81 = 117\)
  2. Longest side: \(11^2 = 121\)

\(117 \ne 121\), so the triangle is not right-angled.

Because \(117 < 121\), the longest side is a bit too long for a right angle: the angle opposite it is bigger than \(90^\circ\).

Builders use this

To check that a corner is square, builders measure 3 m along one wall and 4 m along the other. If the diagonal between the marks is exactly 5 m, the corner is a right angle.

6. Distance between two points

To find the straight-line distance between two points on a grid, draw a right-angled triangle with horizontal and vertical sides. Then use Pythagoras.

Distance on a grid

Find the distance between \(A(-1, 2)\) and \(B(5, 10)\).

Show solution
  1. Horizontal distance: \(5 - (-1) = 6\)
  2. Vertical distance: \(10 - 2 = 8\)
  3. \(AB^2 = 6^2 + 8^2 = 36 + 64 = 100\), so \(AB = 10\)

\(AB = 10\) units

Distance between two points \[ \text{distance} = \sqrt{(\text{change in } x)^2 + (\text{change in } y)^2} \]

7. Problems in context

In word problems, the triangle is often hidden. Always draw a sketch, mark the right angle, and label the sides you know.

A ladder

A 4.5 m ladder leans against a wall. The foot of the ladder is 1.2 m from the wall. How high up the wall does the ladder reach? Give your answer to 3 significant figures.

Show solution
  1. The right angle is where the wall meets the ground. The ladder is opposite it, so 4.5 m is the hypotenuse. Subtract.
  2. \(h^2 = 4.5^2 - 1.2^2 = 20.25 - 1.44 = 18.81\)
  3. \(h = \sqrt{18.81} = 4.3370\ldots\)

\(h = 4.34\) m (3 s.f.)

A journey

A boat sails 12 km north and then 9 km east. How far is it from its starting point?

Show solution
  1. North and east are at right angles, so the direct distance back is the hypotenuse.
  2. \(d^2 = 12^2 + 9^2 = 144 + 81 = 225\)
  3. \(d = \sqrt{225} = 15\)

15 km

Height of an isosceles triangle

An isosceles triangle has two sides of 10 cm and a base of 12 cm. Find its height and its area.

Show solution
  1. The height cuts the base in half, making a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse 10 cm and base 6 cm.
  2. \(h^2 = 10^2 - 6^2 = 100 - 36 = 64\), so \(h = 8\) cm.
  3. Area \(= \tfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 12 \times 8 = 48\)

Height 8 cm, area 48 cm²

8. Two-step problems

Some diagrams contain two right-angled triangles that share a side. Find the shared side first, then use it in the second triangle.

Two triangles

In the diagram, \(AD\) is perpendicular to \(BC\). \(AB = 13\) cm, \(BD = 5\) cm and \(DC = 9\) cm. Find the length \(AC\).

Show solution
  1. Triangle \(ABD\): the hypotenuse is \(AB = 13\). \(AD^2 = 13^2 - 5^2 = 169 - 25 = 144\), so \(AD = 12\) cm.
  2. Triangle \(ADC\): the hypotenuse is \(AC\). \(AC^2 = 12^2 + 9^2 = 144 + 81 = 225\).
  3. \(AC = \sqrt{225} = 15\)

\(AC = 15\) cm

  2. A right-angled triangle has a hypotenuse of 13 cm and a perimeter of 30 cm. Find the lengths of the other two sides.
  3. Start with a right-angled triangle with shorter sides 1 and 1. Use its hypotenuse as one shorter side of a new right-angled triangle, with the other shorter side 1. Keep going, building a spiral.

    2. Expand \((m^2 - n^2)^2 + (2mn)^2\) and show it equals \((m^2 + n^2)^2\).

Show answers
  1. Base diagonal \(\sqrt{900 + 1600} = 50\) cm. Rod \(= \sqrt{50^2 + 120^2} = \sqrt{16\,900} = 130\) cm.
  2. The other two sides add to \(17\) and their squares add to \(169\): 5 cm and 12 cm.

Common mistakes

Adding instead of subtracting

If you already know the hypotenuse, you are finding a shorter side, so subtract. Check: your answer must be shorter than the hypotenuse.

Forgetting the square root

\(c^2 = 100\) means \(c = 10\), not \(100\). The last step is always a square root.

Choosing the wrong hypotenuse

The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle, not just "the sloping side". In a tilted triangle, find the right-angle box first.

Adding the sides, not the squares

\(c = 3 + 4 = 7\) is wrong. Square first: \(c^2 = 9 + 16 = 25\), so \(c = 5\).

Summary

  • Pythagoras' theorem only works in right-angled triangles.
  • The hypotenuse \(c\) is opposite the right angle and is the longest side.
  • \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\).
  • Finding the hypotenuse: add the squares, then square root.
  • Finding a shorter side: subtract the squares, then square root.
  • If \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\) for the three sides, the triangle is right-angled.
  • In problems, sketch the triangle and mark the right angle first.
