Year 9 · Measurement

Pythagoras' theorem

What you'll learn

  • I can find the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle.
  • I can use Pythagoras' theorem to find the length of the hypotenuse.
  • I can use Pythagoras' theorem to find a shorter side.
  • I can decide whether a triangle is right-angled from its side lengths.

Key ideas

Key idea

Pythagoras' theorem only works for right-angled triangles.

The hypotenuse is the longest side. It is always opposite the right angle.

Pythagoras' theorem \[ a^2 + b^2 = c^2 \]

The squares of the two shorter sides add up to the square of the hypotenuse.

Why it works

Draw a square on each side of a 3, 4, 5 triangle. The two smaller squares have a total area equal to the big square.

Worked examples

Finding the hypotenuse

Find the length \(c\).

Show solution
  1. \(c\) is opposite the right angle, so it is the hypotenuse. Add the squares.
  2. \(c^2 = 6^2 + 8^2 = 36 + 64 = 100\)
  3. \(c = \sqrt{100} = 10\)

\(c = 10\) cm

Finding a shorter side

Find the length \(a\).

Show solution
  1. The hypotenuse is 13 cm, so \(a\) is a shorter side. Subtract the squares.
  2. \(a^2 = 13^2 - 5^2 = 169 - 25 = 144\)
  3. \(a = \sqrt{144} = 12\)

\(a = 12\) cm

A problem in context

A 4.5 m ladder leans against a wall. The foot of the ladder is 1.2 m from the wall. How high up the wall does the ladder reach? Give your answer to 3 significant figures.

Show solution
  1. The ladder is opposite the right angle (where the wall meets the ground), so 4.5 m is the hypotenuse. Subtract.
  2. \(h^2 = 4.5^2 - 1.2^2 = 20.25 - 1.44 = 18.81\)
  3. \(h = \sqrt{18.81} = 4.3370\ldots\)

\(h = 4.34\) m (3 s.f.)

Is it right-angled?

A triangle has sides 7 cm, 24 cm and 25 cm. Is it a right-angled triangle?

Show solution
  1. If it is right-angled, the longest side (25 cm) is the hypotenuse.
  2. Shorter sides: \(7^2 + 24^2 = 49 + 576 = 625\)
  3. Longest side: \(25^2 = 625\)

They are equal, so yes, the triangle is right-angled.

If the two numbers were different (for example, sides 6, 9 and 11 give \(36 + 81 = 117\) but \(11^2 = 121\)), the triangle would not be right-angled.

Common mistakes

Common mistake

Adding when you should subtract. If you already know the hypotenuse, you are finding a shorter side, so subtract. Check: your answer must be shorter than the hypotenuse.

Common mistake

Forgetting the square root. \(c^2 = 100\) means \(c = 10\), not \(100\).

Try these

Give answers to 3 significant figures where they are not whole numbers.

  1. A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 9 cm and 12 cm. Find the hypotenuse.
  2. A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 5 m and 7 m. Find the hypotenuse.
  3. The hypotenuse is 17 cm and one shorter side is 8 cm. Find the other side.
  4. The hypotenuse is 10 m and one shorter side is 7 m. Find the other side.
  5. A rectangle is 20 cm long and 15 cm wide. How long is its diagonal?
  6. Is a triangle with sides 8 cm, 15 cm and 17 cm right-angled? Show your working.
  7. Challenge: Find the distance between the points \((1, 2)\) and \((7, 10)\).
Show answers
  1. \(\sqrt{81 + 144} = \sqrt{225} = 15\) cm
  2. \(\sqrt{25 + 49} = \sqrt{74} = 8.60\) m
  3. \(\sqrt{289 - 64} = \sqrt{225} = 15\) cm
  4. \(\sqrt{100 - 49} = \sqrt{51} = 7.14\) m
  5. \(\sqrt{400 + 225} = \sqrt{625} = 25\) cm
  6. \(8^2 + 15^2 = 64 + 225 = 289 = 17^2\), so yes.
  7. Across 6, up 8: \(\sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10\) units
